## 3. Backups

1. **42.4% used a prohibited drug in one RCT.** In a 92-patient diabetes trial, drug-level testing later found undisclosed prohibited metformin use in **39/92 participants (42.4%)**; the authors concluded it introduced a confounder that **invalidated the trial results**. Extremely strong failure-case example, but **not a prevalence estimate across trials**. *Clinical Therapeutics*, 2017. ([PubMed][5])

2. **45.5% of 25,829 trial calls triggered intervention.** Across **12 diabetes clinical trials**, pharmacists made 25,829 participant calls; 11,765 calls had ≥1 intervention, affecting **3,573 patients (92.3%)**. Interventions included **3,341 medication-use protocol violations (12.9%)** and **1,630 concurrent-medication issues (5.9%)**. *Clinical Therapeutics*, 2017. ([PubMed][6])

3. **Manual transcription cost sites ~8 hours/patient/study.** In a Lilly–MSK–Yale proof of concept spanning **seven oncology protocols**, direct electronic data exchange reduced site effort by **8 hours per patient per study**, queries by **2.5 per patient per visit**, and measured transcription errors from **6.7% to 0%**. Peer-reviewed *Journal of the Society for Clinical Data Management*, 2021. ([Clinical Data Management Journal][7])

4. **Queries can consume hours per participant.** A prospective comparison across **6 randomized trials / 126 patients** found targeted SDV produced **587 additional queries**; assuming 30 minutes to handle each query, that represented **294 staff-hours, or 2.4 ± 1.7 hours per patient**. *British Journal of Clinical Pharmacology*, 2019. ([PubMed Central (PMC)][8])

5. **Post-hoc monitoring reached $6.1M for 333 patients.** In I-SPY COVID, retrospective SDV of 333 participants consumed an estimated **61,073 person-hours and $6.1 million**, including **1,461 site hours responding to queries**; cost worked out to **$4,862 per data-field error identified**. *Communications Medicine*, 2025. ([Nature][9])

## 5. Confidence notes

**High confidence:** the **32.8% deviation rate** is peer-reviewed Tufts CSDD research using data supplied by 20 sponsors/CROs. Caveat: protocols were approved **2013–2018**, the sample was a convenience sample, and the figure covers *all* protocol deviations, not medication-specific deviations. ([PubMed Central (PMC)][1])

**Moderate confidence / highly relevant:** the **20 prohibited-conmed flags in 95 visits** is peer-reviewed and directly matches Reconmed's problem, but it is a **small, single-center retrospective pilot**. The authors explicitly say the prohibited-medication deviations were *potential* because they could not confirm retrospectively that each listed medication was actually being taken. ([PubMed Central (PMC)][2])

**High confidence on magnitude, careful on interpretation:** **$40K/day** comes from Tufts CSDD's analysis of 447 actual protocol budgets. It is the **average direct cost of keeping a Phase II/III trial running for another day**, not proof that one conmed discrepancy itself causes a one-day delay. Do not phrase it as “each medication error costs $40K.” ([Tufts CSDD][3])

**Authoritative:** the FDA 2026 conmed guidance is **final**, not draft, though specifically written for oncology trials. ICH E6(R3) and FDA electronic-record guidance provide the broader data-integrity/audit-trail basis. ([U.S. Food and Drug Administration][12])

I would **not use the frequently repeated “$100 per data query” claim on the main slide**. A peer-reviewed ASPREE paper mentions it as a conventional estimate, but cites prior literature rather than establishing it itself; ASPREE's own optimized system actually resolved queries for **$8–10 each**. ([PubMed Central (PMC)][13]) Similarly, I did **not find a defensible modern primary estimate for “cost per protocol deviation” or “cost per CAPA”** that I would put in front of judges as a universal figure.

## And one general-medication-safety graph

This is **not clinical-trial-specific**, so I'd only use it as safety context.

WHO reports medication discrepancies affect **almost every patient moving across transitions of care**, and cites evidence that **11–59% of medication discrepancies** may have potential for harm. The MARQUIS medication-reconciliation work estimated **0.45 potentially harmful discrepancies per patient**. ([World Health Organization][11])

WHO's broader global estimate is:

**$42 billion/year** associated with medication errors worldwide. ([World Health Organization][12])

And WHO's 2024 policy brief says medicines and therapeutic options account for **nearly 50% of preventable harm in medical care**. ([World Health Organization][13])

Again: **healthcare generally, not clinical trials.**
